# Standard Scope And Evidence

Read this reference when formal ASD-STE100 compliance is requested, when explaining the standard, or when checking whether evidence supports a compliance claim.

## Evidence Basis

ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English is a controlled natural language and a standard for technical documentation. It consists of writing rules and a controlled dictionary. ASD owns the standard; the Simplified Technical English Maintenance Group (STEMG) maintains it.

Public official guidance checked on 2026-10-04:

- [Official website](https://www.asd-ste100.org/): identifies Issue 9, January 15, 2025.
- [About STE](https://www.asd-ste100.org/about_STE.html): describes the rules, dictionary, technical terminology, and Issue 9.
- [Official FAQ](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_faq.html): supplies selected word and grammar examples, terminology sources, and limits of checking tools.
- [Official copy request](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html): route to obtain the free official PDF. This link is navigation, not authorization to submit a form or personal information.

This skill summarizes public guidance and supplies original clarity examples. It does not bundle the full standard or dictionary. The complete Issue 9 document was not obtained or reviewed when this skill was authored. Recheck edition-specific facts when the task requires a different or current edition.

## Choose The Review Level

### Principles-Based Editing

Apply clarity principles to the requested artifact and language. For English prose, also use [English vocabulary and usage](english-vocabulary.md), within its stated evidence limits.

Describe the result as STE-inspired or principles-based when the distinction matters. Do not imply that applying the summary verifies compliance. Applying these principles to a non-English document does not make it formally STE-compliant.

### Formal Compliance Review

Keep a user's explicit compliance requirement. Establish:

1. The requested edition and any governing document requirements.
2. Access to that edition's complete writing rules and dictionary through authorized materials.
3. The type of text and the requirements and exceptions that apply to it.
4. Supported project technical nouns and technical verbs, with their terminology sources.
5. The actual reviewed document scope, findings, unresolved terms or rules, and protected-text conflicts.

Check all applicable requirements rather than only the examples in this bundle. Use rule identifiers and word approvals only when verified in the relevant edition.

If the full standard or required evidence is unavailable, complete the supported edits or partial review, preserve unresolved technical facts, and report that formal compliance remains unverified. Ask for missing materials only when they are necessary to complete the user's requested review. Do not substitute a summary review for that requirement without disclosing the limitation.

Checking tools can assist review but do not replace the standard. The official FAQ states that ASD and STEMG do not endorse or certify providers of tools claimed to be fully compliant. Do not claim official endorsement or certification for this skill, a checker, or its output.

## Report Only The Relevant Evidence

For a formal review, identify the edition, evidence used, scope checked, findings, and unverified requirements. Keep protected safety wording and project obligations intact; report any conflict that requires the wording owner's decision.

For ordinary drafting, return the requested text. Add a short qualification only if compliance could otherwise be inferred or a missing technical fact affects use.
