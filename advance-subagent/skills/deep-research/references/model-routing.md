# Model Profiles and Research Routing

## Model Profiles

Choose only `fast`, `default`, or `high`. A profile is an indivisible model-and-effort setting, separate from role and authority.

| Profile | Binding | Use when instructions do not specify a choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fast` | Exact model and effort pair from applicable instructions | Narrow, repeatable work with direct verification |
| `default` | Inherit; omit both model and reasoning effort from spawn | No justified adjustment to the inherited setting |
| `high` | Exact model and effort pair from applicable instructions | Complex reasoning, conflicting evidence, or high error cost |

Follow applicable task or repository profile selections and defaults first. Otherwise select by task demands, uncertainty, verification, and cost. Length or file count alone does not justify `high`. Do not bind roles permanently to profiles.

Resolve concrete pairs from current user or project instructions; do not maintain model IDs, family rankings, or default effort values here. If instructions use combined shorthand, match it to a supported model ID and effort. Do not send the combined shorthand as a model ID or guess an ambiguous split.

Before spawning, check that the selected pair is defined and supported by the current tool. Keep both values together. Do not mix profiles, adjust effort independently, or choose an unconfigured combination. A decision model may advise only among defined, available profiles; a new pair requires an explicit instruction redefining a profile.

If a pair is undefined, unavailable, or has unsupported effort, do not silently repair it. Use another instruction-permitted profile, inheritance, or direct execution only when it meets the role contract. A mandatory profile must not be substituted without authorization. If no valid route remains, report the missing definition or capability. Do not install tools or edit configuration to create a route.

Record the selected profile, why it fits, and either the explicit pair or `inherited`. Do not guess undisclosed inherited values. Report substitutions, unexecuted work, and validation limits.

## Research Roles

- The lead owns scope, definitions, conflict resolution, sensitive inference, source verification, synthesis, and the final report.
- Researchers inspect primary sources for one bounded subquestion or alternative approach and return evidence and counterevidence.
- Structured extractors handle fixed fields and missing-value rules. Flag ambiguity with source passages. They do not own causality, legal interpretation, final confidence, or recommendations. Stop and escalate sample-validation failures.

## Two Delegation Shapes

Both shapes require read-only work, fixed questions, scope, dates, and output contracts; greater research benefit than coordination cost; and lead verification of material claims and citations. Do not delegate simple lookups, short investigations, external writes, or decisions by worker majority.

- **Subquestion coverage:** At least three non-overlapping streams can start independently. Fix shared definitions before dispatch; do not start work with unresolved prerequisites.
- **Alternative exploration:** Two fresh researchers by default examine the same question through distinct hypotheses, methods, or discovery paths. Share goals, constraints, comparison criteria, per-candidate allowance, and an overall round limit. This mode does not require three disjoint subquestions. Shared verified evidence is allowed; previous worker narratives and preferred answers are not.

## Research Packet and Result

Give each researcher only the bounded question and purpose; included and excluded scope; as-of date, region, definitions, and preferred sources; necessary original sources or verified ledger; assigned approach without a conclusion to defend; profile and selection reason, explicit pair or inheritance; read-only authority; allowance, completion and stop conditions; and the result contract.

Start alternative exploration without inherited conversation. Do not pass prior worker outputs, the lead's suspicion, a preferred answer, or private reasoning.

Require a provisional answer; material findings; exact source URLs and recoverable locations; publication or as-of and access dates; supporting passages; counterevidence and alternative explanations; scope and method limits; confidence and uncertainty; and unanswered items with reasons. Mark inaccessible originals and incomplete checks. Snippets and unsupported claim lists are incomplete results.

## Verification and Continuation

The lead manages overlap and evidence gaps, reopens material sources, and checks citations before accepting claims. Never average worker conclusions.

Compare alternative approaches by directness, applicability, independence, freshness, uncertainty reduction, and further investigation cost. Choose the next research direction, retaining all verified findings and counterevidence from unselected approaches. Keep the baseline if no candidate improves it. Defer an unsupported choice and identify the useful next check.

Continue in a new agent without inherited conversation. Its handoff includes goal, constraints, authority, completion criteria; preserved sources and ledger with verification status; selected direction and verified rationale; conflicts and unknowns; remaining allowance and next action. Apply the same profile selection to the continuation worker. Keep original sources available rather than replacing evidence with old worker conclusions. The lead retains report responsibility.

Fork again only with a new question and remaining allowance. If delegation or fresh execution is unavailable, run the evidence workflow sequentially and disclose the limitation. Do not claim parallel execution or context reset.
